How to Practise

You practise the hard places, not the whole piece — repeating what is right, not what is wrong

Related

Slow PracticeHands SeparateMemorisationRhythm TrainingRecording and Self-Assessment

The biggest mistake in practising fits in one sentence:

Many people "practise" by playing the piece from beginning to end. That is not practice; it is checking.

What you practise is not the whole piece but the hard places.

A three-step method

StepDo thisTest
1 find the difficultiesdivide the piece, mark where it sticksyou can say exactly which beats
2 slow down until you can do it rightslow to the point of no errors (see slow practice)three correct repetitions in a row
3 raise the tempo and consolidatesmall increases, then back to the wholestill correct at the new tempo

Step 1 is the one most often skipped. If you cannot say which beat sticks, the only option is running through from the top — spending most of the time on what you can already play while the difficulty goes unsolved.

The core principle: repeat what is right, not what is wrong

The mechanism deserves stating plainly:

Practising is not "playing it many times" but "repeating the correct action until it is automatic".

How you playResult
one right, one wrong, dozens of timestwo ways learned, one of them wrong
slow enough to be right every time, many timesonly one way becomes automatic

The second row is the effective one — so "slow enough to be right" is not caution but the only way to guarantee you are practising correctly.

Which also explains "getting worse with practice": if the wrong repetitions outnumber the right ones, the wrong version is what becomes automatic.

Three practical tests

TestExplanation
Can you start from any point?If not, you are relying on the momentum of running from the top
Can you play the easy places slowly and still be right?Distinguishes true automation from coasting
Can you join a break?Practise the joins separately (between phrases, between sections)

The first is the most useful: much apparent fluency is held up by the order of memory — start in the middle and it collapses, which shows no single place stands on its own yet.

Diagram: the three steps and the two traps

Listen: repetition in small units

"Slow down until you can do it right" corresponds, aurally, to repeating a short span until it is stable. Use rhythm on the simplest possible repeated shape — the real object of practice is often this four-beat scale.

Common misconceptions

  • "Practising means playing the piece several times." That is checking. What you practise is the difficulty, not the whole.
  • "Slow practice wastes time." It is the only way to guarantee practising correctly (see slow practice).
  • "Getting it right once is enough." Three correct repetitions in a row are the minimum before the correct action is fixed.
  • "Fluency means playing it from start to finish." Real fluency is starting from any point and stopping and resuming at will.
