Minor Seventh

A minor triad plus a minor seventh, whose function depends on tonal position and style

2 min readChordsUpdated 2026-11-02

theorychordharmony

Related

A minor seventh is a minor triad plus a minor seventh:

C minor seventh = C–E♭–G–B♭

It is the gentlest of the four common seventh chords — because it pairs two already-dark elements (a minor third and a minor seventh) and contains no sharp internal clash.

It is the most common one

Stack thirds by scale degree:

ScaleDegrees that produce a minor seventh naturally
majordegrees 2, 3 and 6 (ii7, iii7, vi7) — three of them
natural minordegrees 1, 4 and 5 (i7, iv7, v7) — three of them

Compare the neighbours:

  • major seventh: only 2 in a major scale (degrees 1 and 4)
  • half-diminished: only 1 (degree 7)

Three of the seven diatonic seventh chords in major have minor-seventh quality; that count alone does not establish their frequency in repertoire. In the jazz progression ii7–V7–Imaj7, all three are seventh chords: Dm7–G7–Cmaj7 in C major.

What the seventh does

ChordSeventhTreatment
dominant seventhminor sevenththe seventh usually descends by step; the third is the leading tone
minor seventhminor seventhnormally resolves downward in traditional part writing; freer as a colour chord

In G7–C, the chordal seventh F normally descends to E; the third B is the leading tone that rises to C. In traditional part writing, the seventh of a minor seventh chord also normally resolves downward. Jazz and pop allow freer treatment when the chord is used as a colour.

Common misconceptions

  • "The word minor makes it dissonant." It is the gentlest of the four, precisely because it has no tritone.
  • "Every minor seventh must resolve." Traditional part writing normally resolves chordal sevenths downward; jazz and pop treatment also depends on function and style.
  • "A minor seventh is derived from a minor triad." More accurately, it — like the others — arises from stacking thirds on a scale.
  • "More complex chords are more unstable." Instability comes from specific intervals (especially the tritone), not from the number of notes. Ninths and thirteenths can be perfectly stable.
