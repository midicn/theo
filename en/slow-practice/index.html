Slow Practice

The point of slow practice is not slowness but giving the correct action a chance to repeat

3 min readPerformanceUpdated 2026-09-25

theoryperformancepractice

Related

Slow practice is the most widely taught and the most easily misused tool. Start with its purpose:

The point is not slowness but giving the correct action a chance to repeat.

This is the same mechanism as under how to practise: practise means repeating the correct action until it is automatic. Only at a speed that eliminates errors is the correct action the one being repeated.

How slow

One test decides it: slow enough that you make no errors at all.

SpeedResult
still making errorsyou are repeating errors; with more wrong than right repetitions, the wrong one becomes automatic
no errors at allthe correct action is repeated, which is what practice is
so slow the action deformsyou are practising a different action (see below)

Whether you are practising the same action decides everything. Much slow practice is wasted here.

The commonest error: the action changes when you slow down

Slow practice must keep the same action shape as the real performance. This is easily overlooked:

Wrong way to slow downWhy it fails on speeding up
lifting the fingers very highat speed there is no time to lift that high
pressing too hardthe effort distribution at speed is entirely different
breaking the action into several stepsthose steps do not exist at speed
altering the wrist positionthe whole chain of force changes

Once the action changes, it is no longer "the same thing done slowly"; it is a different thing. So ask not "have I slowed down" but "is this the action I use at speed?"

A practical self-check: after a few slow repetitions, play at a moderate tempo. If new problems appear at once, the slow action was not the same action.

Three concrete uses

UseExplanation
establish the correct actionthe primary use
strip away muscle memoryat slow speed momentum fails and the real memory shows (see memorisation)
see the structure and the voicesonly at a slow tempo do harmony and voice leading become audible

Many people believe they have memorised a piece, then find at a slow tempo that they have not. What carried them was the momentum of speed.

Working with the metronome

A metronome is not required for slow practice, but it helps in two situations:

SituationUse
checking whether you really have slowed downset a tempo slower than you imagine
catching secret accelerandohard passages tend to speed up unnoticed

The metronome is a calibration device (see metronome and BPM); it need not run through the session. Follow it throughout and attention shifts from the action to the beat.

Diagram: two kinds of slow practice

Listen: structure at a slow tempo

The third use of slow practice is seeing the structure. Use rhythm on the same pattern at a slow and a fast tempo — at the slow tempo each note is separately audible, which is exactly why slow practice reveals structure.

Common misconceptions

  • "Slow practice is for accuracy." More precisely, it is for giving the correct action a chance to repeat.
  • "The slower the better." Once the action deforms, it is useless. The issue is not speed but whether the action matches the real one.
  • "Hand shape and effort do not matter when slow." The opposite: slow practice is exactly where hand shape and effort are settled — get them wrong and the session is wasted.
  • "Always practise slowly with the metronome." The metronome is for calibration; playing with it throughout moves attention from the action to following the beat.
