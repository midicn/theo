Cadence

Harmonic closure at phrase boundaries, including authentic, plagal, half and deceptive cadences

2 min readHarmonic ProgressionUpdated 2026-11-02

theoryharmonyform

Related

Harmonic FunctionTonal CentreDominant SeventhMelodySonata Form

If harmony had punctuation, it would be the cadence. A cadence decides whether a phrase has finished or is still hanging.

The four cadences

NameMotionClosurePunctuation
authenticV → Istrongestfull stop
plagalIV → Isoft, with afterglowalso a full stop, lighter in tone
halfany chord → Vhangscomma or semicolon
deceptiveV → vidivertedexclamation mark

The difference reduces to one question: which chord does it land on? Landing on the tonic closes (authentic or plagal); landing on the dominant suspends (half); landing elsewhere surprises (deceptive).

Why the authentic cadence is strongest

An authentic cadence moves from dominant to tonic; the dominant need not be a seventh chord. G–B–D to C–E–G already supplies V–I in C major. With G7, B normally rises to C and F descends to E; in C minor, F can descend a whole tone to E♭. A perfect authentic cadence additionally uses root-position chords and ends with the tonic in the melody; rhythm and phrase placement also affect closure.

The sound of the plagal cadence

IV → I lacks the leading tone's thrust, so it lands more gently — with a sense of the curtain coming down rather than a decisive cut. It is common in church music, hymn endings and rock — reach for it when you want solemn rather than decisive.

What the deceptive cadence is for

V → vi postpones. The music is about to land and instead turns to the relative minor. Composers use it to extend a phrase: a place that should have closed does not, and the passage lengthens — a standard device for enlarging structure in melodic writing.

Cadences and form

Cadences help locate boundaries, but their strength does not by itself determine structural level. Melody, rhythm, texture and changes of key also matter:

Cadence strengthUnit it usually divides
weak (half)a pause inside a phrase
medium (plagal, weak authentic)the end of a phrase
strong (full authentic)the end of a period, a section boundary

Which is why the first step in analysing sonata form is finding the cadences.

Common misconceptions

  • "A cadence means an ending." A half cadence can end a phrase while leaving an expectation of continuation. A cadence is punctuation, not only a full stop.
  • "The plagal cadence is a weaker authentic." It is more like a different tone of voice — solemn, relaxed, with afterglow — rather than insufficient force.
  • "A deceptive cadence is a mistake." It is a deliberate postponement device, commonly used to extend phrases.
  • "Cadences belong to classical music." A pop song returning to the tonic, or resting on V, can be described in exactly the same language.
