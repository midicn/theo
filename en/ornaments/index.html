Ornaments

The signs are fixed; the execution changes with the era — literal reading is often wrong

2 min readNotationUpdated 2026-09-25

theorynotationpractice

Related

Articulation MarksHistorical Performance PracticeCounterpointImprovisationNon-Chord Tone

The signs are clear enough, but one thing must come first:

The signs are fixed; their execution varies greatly with era and region.

So "translating the sign literally" is often not the right reading — exactly as with articulation and dynamics.

Four common signs

SignNameBasic execution
small note before a main noteappoggiaturaapproach the main note from a neighbour above or below
tr (or a wavy line)trillrapid alternation with the upper neighbour
short wavy linemordentone rapid alternation with a neighbour
S-shaped curveturncircling the main note (upper neighbour, main, lower neighbour, main)

Three functions

Ornamentation is not merely "adding flowers"; it does three things:

FunctionExplanation
emphasisan appoggiatura on a strong beat gives the accent to the neighbour before resolving — one of the strongest emphases available
flowornamenting a long note keeps the music from stalling
leaving spacethe notation gives a contour and hands the detail to the performer (see improvisation)

One common mistake is to treat the appoggiatura as "a quick note before the real one". In the Baroque and Classical periods it often takes half the main note's value, and harmonically it works as a non-chord tone (see non-chord tones).

One historical point

Ornament execution changed a great deal with the era. The same sign can mean different things in different periods:

PeriodSituation
Baroquesigns are terse, much depends on convention and harmony; performers also added ornaments of their own
Classicalthe length of appoggiaturas and the start and end of trills follow clearer conventions (still regional)
after the Romantic eraornaments are often written out as actual notes, so the signs dwindle

That answers why Baroque ornaments are so hard to play. They were never meant to be read literally; they are a cue plus a convention.

Gongche notation works the same way: a frame plus a cue (see gongche notation). Different traditions make different choices about how much to write and how much to leave open.

Diagram: the sign fixed, the execution fluid

Listen: ornamented and plain

Ornamentation is rapid motion between a main note and a neighbour. Use interval to hear a minor second, the commonest ornament interval. Imagine it alternating quickly on the main note: that is the core of trills and mordents.

Common misconceptions

  • "Ornaments are just decoration." They carry emphasis, flow and open space; a Baroque appoggiatura even functions as a non-chord tone.
  • "Ornament execution is fixed." It varies greatly by era and region; a literal reading is often not what was played.
  • "Modern scores have no ornaments." After the Romantic era they are usually written out as notes; the twentieth century has its own notations (see contemporary notation).
  • "Ornaments can be ignored." In Baroque and Classical works, dropping them changes the melody's actual shape and harmony.
